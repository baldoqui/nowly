Severe Storms Disrupt U.S. Thanksgiving Travel and Postpone Macy's Parade

Thursday, November 26, 2026, 18:30 UTC · 5 sources

Powerful storms barreling across the United States on Thursday threatened to disrupt holiday travel for tens of millions of Americans and forced the historic postponement of New York City's Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade.

According to AAA, an estimated 81.5 million people in the United States were expected to travel for the holiday between Thursday and Monday. However, severe weather conditions spanning both coasts and the Midwest created severe hazards for air and highway travelers. High winds and heavy rain threatened coastal flooding along the East Coast, while winter storm warnings brought heavy snow to parts of the Midwest, including Missouri and Illinois.

The adverse weather led to hundreds of flight cancellations and thousands of delays, particularly at East Coast hubs such as Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport and Baltimore/Washington International Airport. Power outages also left over 520,000 homes in the dark across states including Virginia, Missouri, and Maryland, according to PowerOutage.us. On the West Coast, elevated king tides combined with a large Pacific swell caused coastal flooding along Southern California and damaged the Pacifica Pier south of San Francisco.

The severe weather also derailed traditional holiday celebrations. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced the delay of the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade until Saturday due to forecasted sustained winds between 30 and 40 mph with gusts up to 65 mph. The postponement marks the first time in the parade's 100-year history that the event was moved to a day other than Thanksgiving. In Philadelphia, strong winds similarly forced parade organizers to ground giant balloons.

National Weather Service meteorologists reported that while the multi-hazard storm created unusual winter-level impacts for late November, conditions along the East Coast are expected to clear by Saturday, easing return travel for holiday gatherings.
