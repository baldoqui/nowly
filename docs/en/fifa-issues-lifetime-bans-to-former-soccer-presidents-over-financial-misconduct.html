FIFA Issues Lifetime Bans to Former Soccer Presidents Over Financial Misconduct

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

FIFA ethics judges have banned Vincent Cassell, former president of the Montserrat Football Association, and Bassam Adeel Jaleel, former president of the Football Association of Maldives, from all soccer-related activities for life following investigations into financial misconduct and fund misappropriation.

According to official disciplinary announcements from FIFA, Cassell was charged with abuse of position, systemic conflicts of interest, and misappropriation of funds, alongside a fine of 1 million Swiss francs ($1.23 million). Jaleel was expelled for misusing funds from FIFA's COVID-19 Relief Plan for personal gain, which Maldivian media reported were used to buy luxury apartments, leading to a 23-year prison sentence for embezzling $1.2 million. In addition, former Montserrat general secretary Tandica Hughes received a 15-year ban and a 150,000 Swiss franc ($184,000) fine, while former Maldives financial director Mohamed Ageel was banned for 10 years and fined 100,000 Swiss francs ($184,000) for facilitating the misuse of funds using forged documentation.

The sanctions follow years of governance issues in both federations, with Cassell and Hughes having been previously prosecuted by FIFA's ethics committee in 2011 over $40,000 cash payments linked to a Caribbean election bribery scandal. FIFA installed an emergency management team in Montserrat last year citing an exceptional crisis and institutional paralysis, aligning with warnings issued by FIFA President Gianni Infantino at the 2017 Congress that corrupt officials must leave soccer immediately.
