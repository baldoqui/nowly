Global PISA 2025 Results Reveal Widespread Drop in Student Performance Amid AI and Screen Usage Concerns

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

PARIS — The Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development has released the results of its 2025 Programme for International Student Assessment, showing a historical decline in reading and mathematics scores among 15-year-old students across 91 countries and economies.

Across participating nations, average mathematics performance among OECD countries decreased by 22 points between 2015 and 2025, while reading scores dropped by 28 points over the same period. In Latin America, educational systems recorded mixed results against international benchmarks. Mexico registered 388 points in mathematics and 408 points in reading, down seven points each compared to 2022, while its science score rose slightly to 414 points. In response to the findings, Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum questioned evaluating distinct social realities under uniform criteria, while noting that six public schools achieved top performance within the country's national cohort. Chile maintained its position near the top of the Latin American region despite overall drops in reading and mathematics, though 59 percent of its students remained below basic proficiency in mathematics. Argentina recorded historic lows, with only 24 percent of tested students achieving minimum baseline proficiency in mathematics. According to coverage by EFE, experts attributed the multi-year drop in learning outcomes to a complex combination of socioeconomic inequality, reduced sustained reading habits, extreme heat environments affecting classroom concentration, and heavy daily screen time, which averages 3.4 hours per weekday for entertainment across OECD youth.

The PISA 2025 assessment evaluated approximately 760,000 students worldwide, placing a primary focus on science competencies alongside reading and mathematics. The report highlighted that while moderate use of artificial intelligence as a learning aid produced neutral outcomes, daily reliance on AI to perform primary cognitive tasks such as summarizing and problem-solving correlated strongly with lower academic achievement across jurisdictions.
