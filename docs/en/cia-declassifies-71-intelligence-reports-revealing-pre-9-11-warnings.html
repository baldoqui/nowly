CIA Declassifies 71 Intelligence Reports Revealing Pre-9/11 Warnings

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

WASHINGTON — The Central Intelligence Agency has declassified 71 historical President's Daily Brief documents detailing years of warnings provided to the Clinton and Bush administrations regarding Al Qaeda and Osama bin Laden's intentions to strike inside the United States using aircraft.

The newly released collection spans from February 13, 1998, through September 12, 2001, comprising over 100 pages of intelligence assessments provided to high-ranking federal leadership. According to EFE, the documents include a September 10, 1998 report noting that Bin Laden prioritized attacking inside U.S. territory—specifically Washington—and that his network could crash an explosive-laden aircraft into an American city. Subsequent briefings in December 1998 and August 2001 further highlighted hijackings, airport security evasions, and suspicious domestic activities linked to Al Qaeda, reinforcing earlier findings by the 9/11 Commission regarding institutional communication gaps across government agencies.

The declassification marks the largest single release of President's Daily Brief products concerning the September 11 terror attacks to mark their 25th anniversary. While the intelligence reports establish that senior U.S. officials were repeatedly briefed on Bin Laden's intentions and potential attack modes, analysts note the information remained fragmented and lacked specific operational dates and targets necessary to prevent the tragedy that resulted in nearly 3,000 casualties.
