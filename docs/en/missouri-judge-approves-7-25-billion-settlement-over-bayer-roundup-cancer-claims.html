Missouri Judge Approves $7.25 Billion Settlement Over Bayer Roundup Cancer Claims

Sunday, November 22, 2026, 02:00 UTC · 5 sources

A Missouri circuit court judge has given final approval to a nationwide settlement requiring agrochemical giant Bayer to pay up to $7.25 billion over two decades to resolve thousands of U.S. lawsuits alleging its weed killer Roundup caused non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

Under the agreement approved on Friday by St. Louis Circuit Judge Timothy Boyer, compensation will be distributed to thousands of people who have been or will be diagnosed with non-Hodgkin lymphoma following exposure to Roundup prior to February. Individual payouts are expected to average between $10,000 and $165,000, depending on factors such as the age at diagnosis, extent of exposure, and severity of illness. Judge Boyer characterized the agreement as among the largest mass tort personal-injury class action settlements in U.S. history and noted that individual payouts were fair and adequate, adding that rejection of the deal could have pushed Bayer toward bankruptcy protection. The approved settlement includes $600 million designated for attorneys' fees.

Bayer Chief Executive Officer Bill Anderson described the decision as a decisive milestone in the company's strategy to limit legal liabilities from pending and future litigation. While plaintiff representatives such as court-appointed counsel Christopher Seeger welcomed the guaranteed payments for victims who had waited over a decade for resolution, other attorneys criticized the terms. Opposing counsel argued that individual compensation amounts are insufficient and that the agreement locks claimants into restrictive terms for decades while leaving the agricultural product on the market.

The court ruling comes after a U.S. Supreme Court decision earlier this year that favored Bayer by determining federal regulations shield the company from state court lawsuits alleging a failure to warn consumers about glyphosate's cancer risks. Bayer acquired Monsanto and its flagship Roundup product line in 2018, inheriting about 200,000 legal claims. The World Health Organization's International Agency for Research on Cancer classified glyphosate as probably carcinogenic in 2015, whereas the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency has maintained that the chemical presents no risk of concern for cancer when used as directed. Bayer has already removed glyphosate from U.S. residential products, though it continues to market the herbicide for commercial agriculture.
