Xi and Modi Hold Landmark Meeting at New Delhi BRICS Summit to Reset Relations

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

Chinese President Xi Jinping and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi held a pivotal bilateral meeting on Saturday on the sidelines of the BRICS summit in New Delhi, marking a major step toward normalizing relations following years of severe border tensions.

The meeting marked Xi's first visit to India in seven years, coming after a violent 2020 military confrontation in the disputed Himalayan border region that severely strained diplomatic and economic ties between the two nations. In televised opening remarks, Xi expressed satisfaction with resuming direct contact, stating that China and India are partners rather than rivals and represent mutual development opportunities rather than threats. Both leaders agreed to address a wide range of shared concerns, including trade imbalances, supply chain disruptions, and predictable market access, while reaffirming their commitment to a fair and mutually acceptable resolution of the border dispute. Beyond bilateral matters, the broader summit addressed rising international security crises. According to CCTV, Xi called on the BRICS bloc to act as a mediator in the Middle East conflict, warning that escalating warfare does not serve the common interests of the global community. The summit was also attended by Russian President Vladimir Putin and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian, with member states issuing a joint statement condemning attacks on civilian infrastructure and peaceful nuclear facilities.

The BRICS grouping, originally established by Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa, has recently expanded to include Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Indonesia, and the United Arab Emirates. Representing over 40 percent of the global population, the block serves as a key economic and political platform for emerging market nations seeking to strengthen multilateralism and rebalance global governance.
