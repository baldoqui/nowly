Colombian President Issues One-Week Ultimatum for Surrender of Warlord 'Pinocho'

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

BOGOTA — Colombian President Abelardo De la Espriella has ordered the Ministry of Defense and the Ministry of Justice to coordinate the unconditional surrender of Fredy Castillo Carrillo, alias 'Pinocho', within a strict one-week deadline, warning that military force will be used if the fugitive leader fails to comply.

The presidential ultimatum was issued following an interview released by Semana, in which Castillo Carrillo, leader of the Autodefensas Conquistadoras de la Sierra Nevada (ACSN), expressed a willingness to submit to Colombian and U.S. justice under specific conditions, including a legal framework guaranteeing a maximum five-year prison sentence. President De la Espriella publicly rejected any concessions, stating that the government will not tolerate negotiations, delays, or demands, and warned that failure to surrender will result in targeted security operations. Colombia's Ministry of Justice backed the president's stance, clarifying that Castillo Carrillo must formalize his submission through standard institutional channels without shortcuts, privileges, or agreements outside constitutional bounds.

Castillo Carrillo has a lengthy criminal record stemming from his earlier involvement with the United Self-Defense Forces of Colombia (AUC) and subsequent drug trafficking operations, which led to his extradition to the United States in 2008. After returning to Colombia and later being arrested in Spain in 2022, he served as a designated negotiator for the ACSN during peace talks under the administration of former President Gustavo Petro. The current administration has since reactivated his U.S. extradition order and intensified military pressure on the paramilitary group following the recent death of ACSN commander Naín Andrés Pérez Toncel in La Guajira.
