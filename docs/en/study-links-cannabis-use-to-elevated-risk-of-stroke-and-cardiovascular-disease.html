Study Links Cannabis Use to Elevated Risk of Stroke and Cardiovascular Disease

Thursday, November 19, 2026, 21:00 UTC · 1 source

Marijuana use is associated with an increased risk of stroke and cardiovascular disease, even among infrequent users, according to research presented at the American Heart Association Scientific Sessions 2026 in Chicago.

The study analyzed survey data from nearly 443,000 American adults collected through the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System between 2020 and 2024. Researchers found that low-frequency cannabis users had a 40 percent higher chance of reporting a history of stroke, while heavy users faced a 25 percent higher chance. Dr. Aabha Divya, an assistant professor at Tulane University School of Medicine, noted that researchers were surprised to find that the link between cannabis use and cardiovascular disease was not straightforward, as both low-frequency and heavy users showed higher odds of heart disease. Expert Robert L. Page II emphasized that cannabis is emerging as a major contributor to premature heart disease in both young and older adults, though he noted that concurrent tobacco use among many subjects complicates precise risk assessment.

Although the research does not establish a direct cause-and-effect relationship, experts highlighted the need for healthcare professionals to routinely include cannabis use when evaluating patient heart health alongside discussions of tobacco and alcohol consumption.
