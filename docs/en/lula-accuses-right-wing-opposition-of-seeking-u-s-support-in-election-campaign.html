Lula Accuses Right-Wing Opposition of Seeking U.S. Support in Election Campaign

Sunday, September 13, 2026, 00:08 UTC

CURITIBA, Brazil — Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva accused opposition political forces on Saturday of seeking support from Donald Trump and the United States to boost the presidential campaign of Flávio Bolsonaro ahead of the upcoming election.

Speaking to supporters at a campaign rally in Curitiba, the capital of Paraná state, Lula criticized opposition activists for displaying American flags at political events and warned Washington against meddling in internal affairs. Lula asserted that while his administration aims to maintain positive diplomatic relations with Washington, foreign interference in national elections remains unacceptable, according to Xinhua. He emphasized that the choice of Brazil's next leaders belongs exclusively to the nation's citizens.

The comments come amid rising diplomatic and commercial tensions between Brasilia and Washington over public statements from U.S. officials endorsing opposition figures. With the first round of voting scheduled for October 4, opinion polls show the election focusing heavily on Lula, representing the Workers' Party, and Flávio Bolsonaro, candidate for the Liberal Party.
