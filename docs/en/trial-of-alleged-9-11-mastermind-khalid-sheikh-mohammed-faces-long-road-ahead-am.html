Trial of Alleged 9/11 Mastermind Khalid Sheikh Mohammed Faces Long Road Ahead Amid Torture Rulings

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

GUANTANAMO BAY, Cuba — Two decades after U.S. authorities detained alleged Sept. 11 mastermind Khalid Sheikh Mohammed, legal proceedings remain bogged down by disputes over CIA torture, leaving victims' families waiting for resolution while defense lawyers predict a military trial scheduled for 2028 may never take place.

A U.S. military judge recently established June 5, 2028, as the trial date for Mohammed and three co-defendants held at the Guantanamo Bay detention center in Cuba. However, the case faces severe evidentiary hurdles following a late August ruling that prohibited prosecutors from using a 2007 FBI confession, which was deemed tainted because Mohammed was subjected to severe interrogation methods including 183 sessions of waterboarding after his 2003 capture in Pakistan. According to The Washington Post, former prosecutor David Raskin argued in an essay that military commissions have suffered from endless litigation over their legality and predicted that a trial for Mohammed will never be completed. A plea agreement announced in 2024 that would have secured a life sentence while removing the possibility of execution fell apart and was permanently scrapped last year.

Mohammed was transferred to Guantanamo Bay in September 2006 following more than three years in secret CIA custody overseas. An earlier attempt by the Obama administration in 2009 to try Mohammed in a civilian federal court in New York City was abandoned due to security concerns and political opposition. Organizations representing victims' relatives, including 9/11 Families United, continue to insist on a trial that retains the death penalty as an available punishment.
