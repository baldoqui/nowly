Federal Judge Blocks Executive Order Restricting Birthright Citizenship

Thursday, November 26, 2026, 15:45 UTC · 2 sources

A federal judge in Washington, D.C., has issued a preliminary injunction blocking President Donald Trump's executive order restricting birthright citizenship, ruling that the administration cannot legally establish new exceptions to constitutional guarantees for children born on American soil.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper granted the injunction in a class action lawsuit filed on behalf of children who would be denied automatic citizenship under the order signed in August. The ruling prevents the Department of State, the Department of Homeland Security, and the Social Security Administration from enforcing the directive against children covered by the suit. Judge Cooper described the legal case as clear-cut, stating that the Supreme Court has twice confirmed that virtually all children born in the country are U.S. citizens, provided they are subject to U.S. law. White House spokesperson Lauren Bis criticized the decision, labeling Judge Cooper an activist judge attempting to thwart the president's agenda, while maintaining the order aligns with Supreme Court precedent. Cody Wofsy, an attorney with the American Civil Liberties Union representing the plaintiffs, stated that the administration must accept birthright citizenship as established law.

The August directive was the administration's second attempt to limit birthright citizenship, following a Supreme Court decision in June that rejected a broader effort signed in January 2025. The August order targeted children born to certain foreign government employees, children born through commercial surrogacy, and individuals involved in birth tourism. The decision marks the second federal court order against the August policy, following a Maryland federal judge's injunction in September. The legal controversy centers on the Citizenship Clause of the 14th Amendment to the U.S. Constitution and long-standing Supreme Court precedent, including the 1898 United States v. Wong Kim Ark ruling.
