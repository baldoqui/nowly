NPR Correspondent Reflects on Covering Major South American Political and Papal Events

Sunday, November 22, 2026, 05:00 UTC · 5 sources

National Public Radio South America correspondent Carrie Kahn detailed the logistical and editorial challenges of covering two back-to-back major historic events in Latin America, including Brazil's presidential election runoff and a papal visit to Peru, according to an NPR transcript.

Kahn noted that covering the consecutive historic moments represented a career high point while highlighting the distinct public reactions to each event. In Brazil, President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva achieved a historic victory after trailing in the initial October 4 election round to defeat candidate Jair Bolsonaro. Kahn recalled reporting from Bolsonaro's headquarters in Brasilia on election night as journalists awaited the results of the unprecedented comeback.

Following the election, Kahn reported on Pope Leo XIV's arrival in Peru, where the pontiff had previously lived for two decades. The trip took place weeks after the pope underwent surgery to remove a portion of his lung following a cancer diagnosis. Kahn described an emotional public celebration in Peru, contrasting the warm reception with Pope Francis's 2016 trip to Mexico. She reported that Pope Leo XIV emphasized themes of community, civility, and working for the common good.

Reflecting on the broader demands of foreign correspondence, Kahn cited the vast geography of Latin America and the impact of U.S. foreign policy shifts in the region, including actions concerning Venezuela. Kahn, who has covered the continent for over two decades, emphasized the critical role of collaborating with local journalists and producers to report on key regional developments such as organized crime and political changes.
