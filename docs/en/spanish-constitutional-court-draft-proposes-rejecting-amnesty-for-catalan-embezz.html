Spanish Constitutional Court Draft Proposes Rejecting Amnesty for Catalan Embezzlement Charges

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

Conservative Spanish Constitutional Court Magistrate José María Macías has submitted a draft proposal backing the Supreme Court's decision to deny amnesty for embezzlement charges against Junts Secretary General Jordi Turull.

The proposal, accessed by EFE, maintains that the Supreme Court did not commit any manifest error or act arbitrarily when ruling that Catalan leaders gained a personal financial benefit by using public funds instead of their own assets to finance the 2017 independence referendum. Macías argued that avoiding the depletion of personal wealth through the misuse of public money constitutes personal enrichment under the law. However, the draft is expected to be rejected during the full court session scheduled for September 22, as the Constitutional Court's progressive majority favors applying the amnesty to these offences. If rejected, a new rapporteur will be assigned to draft a ruling reflecting the majority view, delaying a final decision until at least October.

The upcoming decision on Turull's appeal will set the precedent for other Catalan independence figures facing embezzlement charges, including former Catalan President Carles Puigdemont. Supreme Court Judge Pablo Llarena is awaiting this Constitutional Court doctrine before deciding whether to lift the outstanding national arrest warrant against Puigdemont. Following the leak of the proposal, Turull publicly questioned Macías's impartiality, citing the magistrate's previous public opposition to the amnesty law during his tenure on the General Council of the Judiciary.
