Caracas Establishes Municipal Indigenous Women's Day During Cultural Gathering

Sunday, September 13, 2026, 00:07 UTC

CARACAS — The Council of the Bolivariano Libertador Municipality hosted an institutional and cultural event on Saturday at Hugo Chávez Park to honor indigenous women as cultural preservers and formally declare September 5 as the Municipal Day of the Indigenous Woman.

The commemoration, titled "Weaving Resistance and Resilience to Reborn: The Indigenous Woman as the Guiding Thread of Ancestral Culture," brought together local officials, ministerial representatives, and indigenous spokeswomen to promote legislative measures that safeguard indigenous rights in urban contexts. During the ceremony, municipal authorities formally read the local decree honoring Aymara heroine Bartolina Sisa and ancestral leaders, aiming to eliminate discrimination and promote active participation for indigenous women in the capital, as reported by Ciudad Ccs. Municipal Council President Yasmin Campos and local leaders presented recognitions to health promoters, youth, and traditional knowledge preservers, while also awarding the "Good Citizen, Liberator Simón Bolívar" Order of Honor to Minister for Indigenous Peoples Clara Vidal, represented by Andreína Pineda.

The gathering featured speeches by prominent indigenous figures, including Deputy Noelí Pocaterra and Jorge Armando Marino, president of the National Institute of Indigenous Languages (Inidi), who emphasized the critical role of preserving native languages and cultural identity. Community representatives highlighted that maintaining ancestral traditions and languages within urban settings remains essential for transmitting indigenous heritage to younger generations.
