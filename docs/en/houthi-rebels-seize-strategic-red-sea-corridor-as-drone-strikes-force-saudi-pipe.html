Houthi Rebels Seize Strategic Red Sea Corridor as Drone Strikes Force Saudi Pipeline Closure

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

Iran-aligned Houthi rebels in Yemen have consolidated control over the Bab el-Mandeb strait and captured the port city of Mokha following a rapid offensive, while drone attacks launched from Iraq forced the shutdown of Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline, deepening a global energy crisis.

The Houthi advance secured control over 5,400 square kilometers along Yemen's western coast, including the strategic port of Mokha and the islands of Perim and Hanish, effectively cutting off Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces from the Red Sea. Concurrently, drone strikes launched from Iraq's Maysan province struck Saudi Arabia's East-West Petroline pipeline, forcing Riyadh to halt operations on the critical bypass route that had mitigated the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered an investigation and removed the local military commander following confirmation that the strikes originated from Iraqi territory, according to The Associated Press. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman appealed directly to U.S. President Donald Trump for military intervention against the Houthis, but Washington declined, with Trump stating that the rebel group had requested to avoid direct conflict with U.S. forces. The combined disruptions at both major regional maritime choke points pushed Brent crude oil prices back up toward 100 dollars per barrel as global shipping routes faced severe restrictions.

The fresh escalation marks a breakdown of the 2022 truce that had paused Yemen's long-running civil war, which previously claimed an estimated 150,000 lives. According to the International Organization for Migration, more than 76,000 people have been displaced in western Yemen since July as military operations intensify, leaving emergency relief agencies facing critical shortages of food, shelter, and medical supplies.
