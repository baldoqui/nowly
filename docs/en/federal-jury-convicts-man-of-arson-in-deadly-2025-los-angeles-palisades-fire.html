Federal Jury Convicts Man of Arson in Deadly 2025 Los Angeles Palisades Fire

Saturday, November 21, 2026, 22:00 UTC · 5 sources

A federal jury in Los Angeles convicted 30-year-old Jonathan Rinderknecht on Friday of arson charges for starting the deadly 2025 Palisades Fire, following a retrial after his initial federal trial ended in a mistrial.

Jurors found Rinderknecht guilty of two federal charges, including destruction of property by means of fire, for igniting a brush fire on Jan. 1, 2025, known as the Lachman Fire. According to federal prosecutors, the initial blaze burned undetected deep within underground root systems before flaring back up on Jan. 7 during high winds. The resulting Palisades Fire killed 12 people and incinerated thousands of homes across Los Angeles and Malibu.

During the retrial, prosecutors presented digital forensic data, cellphone tower records placing Rinderknecht near the trail, and images from fire detection cameras. Investigators also cited evidence of his state of mind prior to the fire, including text messages and searches on an artificial intelligence tool asking if someone could be held responsible for a fire started by cigarettes.

The defense team argued that the government failed to present direct evidence or prove Rinderknecht lit the fire, pointing to witness reports of fireworks on New Year's Eve as an alternative source of ignition. Defense attorney Steve Haney also raised concerns about missing cellular records and presented expert testimony disputing the exact point of origin.

The verdict comes five months after a federal jury deadlocked 10 to 2 in favor of acquittal in June, prompting prosecutors to refile a narrowed indictment. Rinderknecht faces a mandatory minimum sentence of five years and a maximum of up to 20 years in federal prison for each count, with sentencing scheduled for March 17, 2027.
