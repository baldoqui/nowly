May el-Toukhy's 'Woman Unknown' Wins Golden Lion at 83rd Venice Film Festival

Sunday, September 13, 2026, 00:06 UTC

Danish director May el-Toukhy's historical drama 'Woman Unknown' won the prestigious Golden Lion for Best Film on September 12, 2026, as the 83rd Venice Film Festival concluded in Venice, Italy, capping a politically charged edition of the world's oldest film festival.

'Woman Unknown', which follows a domestic worker in post-WWII Denmark struggling to hide her past relationship with a German soldier, earned a double victory as lead actress Mathilde Arcel took home the Volpi Cup for Best Actress. Jury President Maggie Gyllenhaal presented the award to el-Toukhy, making her only the eighth female director to win the festival's top honor. The Silver Lion Grand Jury Prize went to South Korean director Lee Chang-dong for 'Possible Love', his first feature in eight years, which examines economic division and human connection. Russian filmmaker Ilya Khrzhanovsky won the Silver Lion for Best Director for 'DAU', a monumental multi-decade project exploring Soviet totalitarianism through the life of physicist Lev Landau. American actor John Malkovich received the Volpi Cup for Best Actor for his performance as a terminally ill CIA agent in Martin McDonagh's dark comedy 'Wild Horse Nine', according to EFE. Additionally, Israeli filmmakers Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor received the Special Jury Prize for 'NAZA', a secret nocturnal documentary examining Israeli military and intelligence strategy in Gaza through 24 insider testimonies, while Stéphane Brizé, Coralie Amédéo, and Olivier Gorce won Best Screenplay for 'Un bon petit soldat'.

The festival was marked by prominent discussions surrounding gender equity in the film industry after artistic director Alberto Barbera initially included only one female-directed film in the official 20-film competition lineup. Organizers and jury members emphasized that this year's slate underscored the role of international cinema in directly confronting contemporary geopolitics, labor ethics, and historical trauma.
