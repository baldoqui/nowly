Cheaper Materials and Complex Electronics Significantly Shorten Modern Refrigerator Lifespan

Sunday, November 22, 2026, 05:00 UTC · 5 sources

Consumers are facing escalating replacement costs as modern refrigerators fail significantly earlier than older models due to lower-quality manufacturing materials and increasingly complex electronic components.

The Hernandez family of Lehigh Acres, Florida, spent between $3,000 and $4,000 on two separate refrigerators over a five-year period after a unit purchased in 2020 failed in less than three years, according to regional news reports. Alex Radov, a repair technician with OHM Appliance Repair, stated that manufacturers have increasingly replaced durable copper coils with aluminum and cheaper metal alloys that corrode quickly. Furthermore, modern appliances incorporate complex inverter boards and advanced electronics rather than simple control boards and static relays, increasing potential failure points and reducing average appliance lifespan from 20 years to just four or five years.

Technicians noted that appliance manufacturers rarely disclose detailed information regarding compressors or inverter boards to consumers before purchase. Consequently, repair experts advise consumers to preserve and maintain older, simpler appliances whenever feasible to minimize expenses.
