Over 76,000 Displaced in Yemen Amid Escalating Houthi Offensive, IOM Reports

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

GENEVA — At least 76,000 people have been forced to flee their homes in Yemen since July, with displacement numbers rising sharply in recent weeks due to a renewed offensive by Houthi rebels in the western region of the country, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) announced on Saturday.

According to data published by the UN agency's Displacement Tracking Matrix and reported by Europa Press, at least 30,000 people fled within recent days alone as fighting intensified along key routes toward the strategic Bab el-Mandeb Strait. The IOM warned that displacement figures nearly doubled overnight and represent a fourfold increase compared to the previous week. Taiz governorate on the western coast has been the hardest hit, accounting for nearly 50,000 displaced individuals, followed by Lahj with approximately 17,000, Hodeida with over 8,000, and Aden with 1,100. Entire families continue to flee, frequently during the night, leaving behind their homes and livelihoods with no safe destination, while ongoing communication blackouts in combat zones hamper aid workers from assessing immediate humanitarian needs.

Many of the affected families have suffered repeated displacements across Yemen's 12-year conflict, leaving their personal resources exhausted. The latest surge in displacement comes as Houthi rebel militias push toward strategic positions along the Red Sea, a vital corridor for global maritime commerce.
