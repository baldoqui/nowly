Over 10,000 Demonstrate in Dublin Against U.S. President Donald Trump's Ireland Visit

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

More than 10,000 protesters gathered in central Dublin on September 12, 2026, to demonstrate against a two-day informal visit by U.S. President Donald Trump and to voice support for Ireland's traditional policy of military neutrality.

Demonstrators marched from the Garden of Remembrance toward the center of Dublin and the Dáil, the Irish parliament, chanting slogans opposing Trump and criticizing Irish authorities for allowing the visit. The anti-war demonstration, organized under the slogan "Trump, You Are Not Welcome," was called by groups including the Irish Neutrality League and the Irish Anti-War Movement, alongside political parties such as the Labour Party, Sinn Féin, and People Before Profit. Protesters carried Palestinian flags and kufiyas alongside satirical displays, calling for an end to foreign military transit through Irish facilities and accusing the U.S. president of complicity in the Gaza conflict, according to EFE.

Trump arrived in Ireland for a two-day informal stay, holding meetings in Dublin with Irish President Catherine Connolly and Prime Minister Micheál Martin before traveling to Doonbeg to attend the Irish Open at his golf complex. Further demonstrations were also scheduled near Shannon Airport and surrounding the Doonbeg resort throughout the weekend.
