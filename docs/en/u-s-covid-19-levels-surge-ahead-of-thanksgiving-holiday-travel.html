U.S. COVID-19 Levels Surge Ahead of Thanksgiving Holiday Travel

Thursday, November 26, 2026, 16:30 UTC · 1 source

COVID-19 infections and wastewater levels are increasing across the United States as millions prepare to travel for the winter holidays, according to new federal data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

The CDC estimates that COVID-19 infections are growing or likely growing in 34 states as of November 18, with the highest wastewater concentrations detected in the Southeast and Southwest. High virus levels were tracked between November 8 and November 14 in states including Nevada, Utah, Tennessee, and Florida, alongside nearly 20 other states nationwide. The percentage of positive tests rose to 3.66 percent on November 14, up from 3.55 percent the previous week.

The health agency noted that hospitalizations are rising alongside the infection rate. The surge aligns with peak holiday travel projections from the American Automobile Association, which estimates 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home between November 24 and November 30. That total includes 72.9 million people traveling by car and 5.96 million taking domestic flights.

Nearly 8,000 Americans have died following COVID-19 infections this year. The CDC and health organizations recommend updated vaccines targeting current Omicron offshoots for everyone six months and older. The Association of American Medical Colleges advises adults over 65 to receive a shot every six months to lower the risk of severe outcomes such as pneumonia and organ failure. Only 14 percent of U.S. adults reported receiving an updated vaccine by October 31, according to Medical Daily.
