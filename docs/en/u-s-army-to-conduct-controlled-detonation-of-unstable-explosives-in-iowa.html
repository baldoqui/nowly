U.S. Army to Conduct Controlled Detonation of Unstable Explosives in Iowa

Thursday, November 19, 2026, 21:00 UTC · 123 sources

The U.S. Army plans to execute a controlled detonation of approximately five tons of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa, on Friday to eliminate the threat of an accidental blast.

According to official statements and reports from the Iowa Capital Dispatch, the hazardous material became volatile after stabilizing liquid inside storage barrels evaporated over two decades, leaving crystallized chemicals extremely sensitive to shock and friction. The Environmental Protection Agency issued an emergency permit for the operation after ruling out alternative neutralization methods as excessively dangerous. Local authorities advised nearby residents to evacuate or shelter indoors, while several area school districts suspended classes due to potential toxic lead dust plumes that could drift up to 100 miles depending on wind direction. Environmental mitigation measures for the blast include sand and concrete barriers, water suppression sprays, and continuous air quality tracking.

The Environmental Protection Agency and military personnel will evaluate air and soil quality surrounding the Middletown facility during and after the detonation. The Army has committed to conducting environmental cleanup operations if post-event testing reveals lead contamination exceeding federal regulatory thresholds.
