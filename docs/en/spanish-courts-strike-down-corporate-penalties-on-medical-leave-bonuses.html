Spanish Courts Strike Down Corporate Penalties on Medical Leave Bonuses

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

Spanish tribunals, led by the Supreme Court, have increasingly ruled against corporate policies that reduce or eliminate attendance bonuses and profit-sharing payouts for employees who take justified medical leave.

The legal shift follows Spain's 2022 legislation identifying health status as a protected category against employment discrimination. According to a report by Spanish News Outlet, supermarket giant Mercadona distributed 780 million euros in profit sharing among 112,000 workers this year, but faced Supreme Court annulment of collective agreement clauses that penalized employees taking over 30 days of sick leave. The Supreme Court declared clauses discriminatory if medical absences prevented workers from reaching the minimum required active service time to receive bonuses. Legal experts note that courts now view deducting attendance or punctuality rewards for certified medical absences as unlawful, leaving only unjustified absences subject to penalty.

The judicial stance extends beyond private retail to other corporate sectors and public administration, restricting clauses that link attendance metrics to bonus eligibility. Labor law specialists highlight that approved medical leave and caregiver permissions must be treated as effective working hours, avoiding indirect discrimination against workers and caregivers.
