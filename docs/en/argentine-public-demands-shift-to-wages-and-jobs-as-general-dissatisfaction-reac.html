Argentine Public Demands Shift to Wages and Jobs as General Dissatisfaction Reaches 70 Percent

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

A recent survey by the Universidad de San Andrés revealed that 70 percent of Argentines are dissatisfied with the general trajectory of the country, while public anxiety has shifted from inflation toward low wages and job security ahead of the 2027 presidential elections.

According to the study conducted by researcher Diego Reynoso, approval for President Javier Milei's administration currently stands at 35 percent, holding steady for four months after a period of continuous decline between October 2025 and April 2026. In details reported by Agencia NA, Reynoso explained that inflation ceased to be the chief concern after the government addressed high price increases during its first two years in power. However, a new agenda of economic demands has emerged around insufficient salaries and fears of unemployment. On the political front, Axel Kicillof leads preferences among opposition voters in a fragmented landscape, while Milei and Patricia Bullrich remain the top choices among government supporters.

President Milei is seeking re-election in 2027 as his administration navigates changing public demands. Meanwhile, former President Cristina Kirchner remains legally unable to run, leaving opposition voters divided among several potential political figures.
