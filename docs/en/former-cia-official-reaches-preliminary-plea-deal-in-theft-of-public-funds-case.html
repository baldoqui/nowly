Former CIA Official Reaches Preliminary Plea Deal in Theft of Public Funds Case

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

Former CIA official David J. Rush has reached a preliminary plea agreement after being charged with theft of public funds for allegedly inflating his salary fraudulently, according to federal court documents.

A federal judge extended the deadline until October 8 to formally present an indictment, giving prosecutors and defense counsel time to finalize the plea deal and prevent a public trial that could involve complex litigation over classified information. According to an FBI affidavit reported by The Associated Press, Rush allegedly claimed 744 hours of military leave fraudulently after being honorably discharged from the Navy in 2015, and falsely claimed degrees from Clemson University and Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute to inflate his pay. During a search of his home, investigators seized about 300 gold bars valued at over $40 million, nearly $2 million in cash, and 35 luxury watches.

Rush was charged in May with theft of public funds. Although the FBI affidavit noted that the gold bars were obtained from the U.S. government for work-related expenses, Rush's defense attorney stated that the criminal charge is unrelated to the gold bars, characterizing their mention as a sensationalist detail.
