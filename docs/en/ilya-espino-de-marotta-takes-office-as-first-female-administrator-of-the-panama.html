Ilya Espino de Marotta Takes Office as First Female Administrator of the Panama Canal

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

Marine engineer Ilya Espino de Marotta has formally assumed office as the first female administrator in the 112-year history of the Panama Canal during a ceremony in Panama City attended by President José Raúl Mulino.

Espino de Marotta, who began her career at the waterway in 1985 as a shipyard engineer, now leads nearly 9,000 employees overseeing a conduit responsible for approximately 6% of global maritime trade. According to a report by BBC News Mundo, her appointment comes at a crucial period as the canal faces operational constraints caused by severe water shortages and heightened diplomatic tensions between its two primary users, the United States and China. To mitigate persistent drought risks caused by climate change, the Panama Canal Authority plans to initiate construction of a new reservoir on the Indio River in 2027, aiming to protect water supply after drought-related transit restrictions in 2023 reduced canal revenues by roughly $900 million.

Espino de Marotta previously served as Vice President of Engineering, playing a pivotal role in leading the final phase of the canal expansion project completed in 2016. That expansion added a third transit lane for larger vessels and currently generates half of the canal's annual revenue. Since the official transfer of the interoceanic waterway from U.S. administration to Panamanian control 30 years ago, the canal has contributed $31 billion directly to the national treasury, accounting for roughly 10% of Panama's economy.
