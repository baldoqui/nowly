Colombia Restricts Machinery and Drone Imports to Combat Illegal Mining and Smuggling

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella announced strict new border controls on heavy machinery, drone parts, and chemical precursors, limiting their import entry strictly to Cartagena and Bogota as part of a national strategy against illegal mining, drug trafficking, and contraband.

The government measures target critical supplies used by criminal organizations to exploit ecosystems and evade law enforcement. According to Vanguardia, the National Directorate of Taxes and Customs (DIAN) seized more than 30 billion Colombian pesos in smuggled goods and operational drones during the first month of the current administration. President De la Espriella also confirmed a structural reorganization of the Fiscal and Customs Police (POLFA), which will be headed by female officers for the first time, while border and port customs operations will be led by military personnel working in coordination with U.S. agencies including Homeland Security Investigations (HSI).

Government estimates place annual state losses from contraband at nearly 120 trillion Colombian pesos. In addition to physical import restrictions, tax authorities announced an upcoming fiscal monitoring program focused on undeclared foreign cryptocurrency holdings among Colombian tax residents.
