Trump Rules Out USMCA Withdrawal, Highlights Strong Ties With Mexico

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

DUBLIN — U.S. President Donald Trump on Saturday ruled out withdrawing from the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA), affirming that Washington expects to maintain an 'excellent relationship' with Mexico despite mounting trade friction with Canada.

Speaking to reporters following a bilateral meeting with Irish Prime Minister Micheál Martin in Dublin, Trump rejected the idea of abandoning the North American trade pact when questioned about its future. In contrast to his favorable remarks regarding Mexico, Trump intensified his trade rhetoric against Ottawa, accusing Canada of taking advantage of American farmers for 50 years and asserting that the United States does not rely on Canadian products. According to Reuters, U.S. and Mexican negotiators have been accelerating bilateral discussions to resolve key disputes over automotive rules of origin, steel and aluminum tariffs, and non-market foreign investment controls prior to the U.S. midterm elections.

The USMCA replaced NAFTA in July 2020 and retains legal force through 2036. The three participating nations entered a regime of annual reviews after failing to secure a formal 16-year extension earlier this year, while Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum has consistently defended the trilateral structure as beneficial to all North American economies.
