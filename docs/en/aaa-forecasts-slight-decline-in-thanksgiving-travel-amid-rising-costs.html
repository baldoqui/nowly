AAA Forecasts Slight Decline in Thanksgiving Travel Amid Rising Costs

Thursday, November 26, 2026, 16:15 UTC · 5 sources

An estimated 81.5 million Americans are projected to travel at least 50 miles from home during the Thanksgiving holiday period from Nov. 24 through Nov. 30, marking a slight 0.1 percent decline from the previous year as travel expenses increase, according to projections from AAA.

The anticipated drop of about 121,000 travelers would end a three-year stretch of record-high Thanksgiving travel. Automobile travel remains the predominant choice for holiday trips, with AAA expecting 72.9 million people to hit the road, down from 73.1 million last year. Drivers face higher fuel prices, with nationwide gas prices averaging $3.81 per gallon in mid-November compared with $3.08 a year earlier. AAA reported that if prices remain at current levels, fuel costs could mark a holiday record. Air travel is also expected to contract slightly, with domestic flights projected at 5.96 million passengers, a 0.2 percent decrease from last year. Average round-trip airfares booked through AAA rose 17 percent year-over-year to $800. In contrast, alternative travel options including buses, trains, and cruises are expected to rise 4.1 percent to roughly 2.6 million travelers. Peak roadway traffic is projected for the Tuesday and Wednesday before Thanksgiving and the Sunday and Monday following the holiday, with major metro areas such as Boston, Los Angeles, New York, and Washington expected to experience heavy congestion.

AAA prepared its holiday projections in mid-October before a Nov. 13 Federal Aviation Administration ground stop caused nationwide flight disruptions due to a telecommunications failure involving a newly installed router. The FAA subsequently paused router installations, and AAA indicated the disruption is unlikely to impact Thanksgiving travel unless another system outage occurs.
