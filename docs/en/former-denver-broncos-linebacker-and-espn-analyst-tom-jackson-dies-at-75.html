Former Denver Broncos Linebacker and ESPN Analyst Tom Jackson Dies at 75

Sunday, November 22, 2026, 19:45 UTC · 5 sources

Former Denver Broncos All-Pro linebacker and longtime ESPN broadcaster Tom Jackson died on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, at the age of 75, his family and the network announced on Sunday.

Jackson's death was revealed by his longtime on-air partner Chris Berman at the beginning of ESPN's Sunday NFL Countdown show, per the wishes of Jackson's family. A cause of death was not publicly released. Berman described Jackson as a vital figure and his closest friend both professionally and personally, while ESPN Chairman Jimmy Pitaro remembered Jackson as one of the most influential and beloved personalities in the network's history. To honor his legacy and connection to Denver, the ESPN studio crew wore orange ties during the broadcast. The Denver Broncos released a statement stating the organization was heartbroken by the passing of the Ring of Fame linebacker, calling him a fierce competitor and emotional leader. NFL Commissioner Roger Goodell also offered condolences, highlighting Jackson's toughness, passion, and credibility across his playing and television careers.

Selected by Denver in the fourth round of the 1973 NFL Draft out of the University of Louisville, Jackson played 14 seasons as a cornerstone of the Broncos' famed Orange Crush defense. He appeared in 191 regular-season games, earned three Pro Bowl selections, was named a first-team All-Pro in 1977, and helped guide the franchise to its first two Super Bowl appearances. Following his retirement from football in 1986, Jackson joined ESPN in 1987, where he co-hosted iconic highlights programs NFL Primetime and Sunday NFL Countdown alongside Berman for nearly three decades. Jackson was inducted into the Broncos' Ring of Fame in 1992 and received the Pro Football Hall of Fame's Pete Rozelle Radio-Television Award in 2015. He is survived by his wife, Jennifer, and his daughters, Taylor and Morgan.
