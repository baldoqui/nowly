Spanish Opposition Rejects Official's Resignation as Insufficient Amid Ceuta Migration Crisis

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

VIGO, Spain — Spain's main opposition People's Party on Saturday rejected the resignation of a government official as insufficient accountability for the handling of a major border crisis in the autonomous enclave of Ceuta, where thousands of migrants recently crossed from Morocco.

Speaking to reporters in Vigo, PP national spokesperson Borja Sémper characterized the resignation of Gonzalo Sanz, chief of staff to the central government delegate in Ceuta, as 'a joke' given the magnitude of the emergency. Sémper accused the administration of Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez of failing to act on advance intelligence warnings provided by Spain's CNI agency prior to the mass border crossings. According to Servimedia, Sémper also responded to recent criticism from Rabat aimed at the opposition, warning that a future conservative government would hold a firm position against any foreign infringement on Spain's sovereign territories, including Ceuta, Melilla, and the Canary Islands.

The ongoing fallout in Ceuta has heightened political tensions in Madrid, where opposition parties are pressing for full judicial investigations into government conduct. Local authorities in Ceuta report thousands of migrants remaining in the enclave, accompanied by a sharp rise in security interventions following the border breach.
