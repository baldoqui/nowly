Former Army Supervisor Rebukes Maryland Governor Wes Moore in Military Service Dispute

Sunday, November 22, 2026, 19:15 UTC · 5 sources

Retired U.S. Army Lt. Col. James Gottschling has issued a sharp rebuke of Maryland Governor Wes Moore after the governor publicly referenced his former supervisor's mental health during an interview addressing discrepancies in Moore's military deployment record, according to reports from Spotlight on Maryland.

The escalation followed an interview released Nov. 15 on the Noosphere platform with former broadcaster Chuck Todd. When pressed about Gottschling's statements challenging Moore's accounts of leading soldiers in combat, Moore stated that he prayed for Gottschling and noted that mental health challenges are real, though he offered no factual basis or diagnosis. Gottschling, who supervised Moore day-to-day during his 2005 to 2006 deployment at Forward Operating Base Salerno in eastern Afghanistan, accused the governor of attempting to undermine his credibility rather than refuting the facts. Gottschling and a second retired officer, Lt. Col. James Patton, maintain that Moore served effectively as a staff information operations officer but did not command troops or participate in direct-fire firefights. The dispute also encompasses Moore's receipt of a Bronze Star Medal in 2024, nearly two decades after the deployment, which Gottschling stated he was not consulted on and would not have supported over Army rules against dual awards for identical service periods.

Moore defended his military record by pointing to his Combat Action Badge and a highly favorable 2006 officer evaluation report written by Gottschling. Records indicate the Combat Action Badge was awarded following a December 2005 indirect-fire rocket attack on FOB Salerno that resulted in no casualties. The governor's office has previously described media scrutiny of Moore's service as politically motivated, citing his national political profile and potential presidential ambitions, while Gottschling maintains his statements are strictly based on military records and personal oversight.
