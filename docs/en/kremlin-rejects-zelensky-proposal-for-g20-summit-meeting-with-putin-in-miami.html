Kremlin Rejects Zelensky Proposal for G20 Summit Meeting with Putin in Miami

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

MOSCOW — Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov on Saturday rejected a proposal by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky to hold a direct meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin during the G20 summit in Miami, asserting that any bilateral encounter must take place in Moscow.

Responding to Zelensky's statements expressing readiness to talk if both leaders attend the December summit in the United States, Peskov characterized a Miami meeting as impossible and reiterated Russia's standing demand for the Ukrainian leader to travel to the Russian capital. Russian presidential adviser Yuri Ushakov added that Putin's travel to the summit has not yet been discussed internally and that preparations remain purely at the technical level, according to Agence France-Presse. The diplomatic maneuvering coincides with renewed U.S. mediation efforts, with special envoy Jared Kushner stating via videoconference at the Yalta European Strategy forum in Kyiv that Washington hopes to announce new negotiation steps in the coming weeks following diplomatic visits to both capitals.

Direct negotiations between Moscow and Kyiv remain stalled, as Ukraine maintains that top-level talks are necessary to address key issues such as territorial sovereignty, while Russia insists a presidential summit should only take place to sign pre-negotiated agreements.
