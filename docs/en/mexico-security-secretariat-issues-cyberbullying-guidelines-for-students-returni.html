Mexico Security Secretariat Issues Cyberbullying Guidelines for Students Returning to School

Sunday, September 13, 2026, 00:07 UTC

Mexico's Ministry of Security and Protection of Citizens issued a series of recommendations aimed at preventing cyberbullying among children and adolescents as students return to school.

Released through the Cyber Intelligence, Research, and Technological Operations Unit, the guidance warns that digital platforms—including social media, messaging services, and online video games—can expose minors to harassment such as insults, threats, unauthorized photo sharing, and fake account creation. The agency urged parents and guardians to maintain open communication with children, establish age-appropriate technology limits, and prevent the sharing of sensitive personal information, according to a report by AIMX. Additionally, the SSPC advised families not to delete evidence if cyberbullying occurs, recommending that they preserve screenshots, messages, and links to document any aggression.

The campaign coincides with the return-to-school period across Mexico, where public security authorities are promoting their official Ciberguía resource to help families identify digital risks and foster respectful behavior in online environments.
