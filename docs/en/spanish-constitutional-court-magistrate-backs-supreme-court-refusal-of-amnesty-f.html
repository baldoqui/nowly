Spanish Constitutional Court Magistrate Backs Supreme Court Refusal of Amnesty for Catalan Leader

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

Spanish Constitutional Court Magistrate José María Macías has issued a draft ruling supporting the Supreme Court's decision to exclude former Catalan minister Jordi Turull from the nation's amnesty law over embezzlement charges.

In the draft report scheduled for plenary debate on September 22, Macías concluded that the Supreme Court's refusal to apply amnesty for the misuse of public funds adhered to established legal doctrine and was neither arbitrary nor unpredictable. According to report details accessed by EFE News Agency, Macías affirmed that personal financial benefit encompasses saved expenses that would otherwise have been paid with private funds. The draft also noted that a July ruling by the Court of Justice of the European Union backing the amnesty law cannot be applied retrospectively to invalidate earlier Supreme Court orders. If the Constitutional Court's majority rejects the proposal as expected, a new magistrate will be appointed to draft a revised opinion.

Turull's appeal marks the first legal challenge regarding the application of the amnesty law for Catalan independence leaders to reach the Constitutional Court. The resolution is expected to set a precedent that will directly influence other pending cases, including that of former Catalan president Carles Puigdemont.
