At Least Two Dead After Building Collapses on Bolivian Family Home in São Paulo

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

At least two women died early Saturday after a multi-story building under construction collapsed onto an adjacent home housing a Bolivian migrant family in the Penha neighborhood of eastern São Paulo, Brazil.

Emergency crews rescued an adult man and a child from the debris, while search and rescue teams deployed heavy machinery, search dogs, and manual bucket operations to clear rubble in fragile structural zones. The collapsed building was an embargoed construction site where no permanent residents lived, though officials indicated two construction workers occasionally slept inside. Bolivian diplomatic authorities confirmed they are providing consular protection and assistance to affected citizens, according to EFE. Civil Defense officials reported that heavy rainfall occurred around the time of the collapse at 2:00 a.m., but technical inspectors are evaluating structural defects to determine if weather was the primary cause.

The Penha neighborhood is a traditional working-class district in the eastern zone of São Paulo, South America's most populous city. Official embargoes on construction projects are typically issued for legal or safety violations, and local authorities have initiated an investigation into compliance and structural management at the site prior to the disaster.
