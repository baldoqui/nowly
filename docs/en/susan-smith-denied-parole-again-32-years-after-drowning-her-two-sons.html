Susan Smith Denied Parole Again 32 Years After Drowning Her Two Sons

Thursday, November 19, 2026, 21:00 UTC · 31 sources

Susan Smith, the South Carolina woman convicted in 1995 of murdering her two young sons by drowning them in a lake, was unanimously denied parole for a second time following a hearing in Columbia, South Carolina, on Thursday.

The seven-member South Carolina Department of Probation, Parole and Pardon Services board voted unanimously to reject release for the 55-year-old inmate, who joined the proceedings virtually from the Leath Correctional Institution. Under state regulations, Smith needed two-thirds support from the board to secure parole, an outcome rarely granted given the board's historical 4% approval rate that primarily favors non-violent offenders.

During her testimony, Smith expressed remorse and asked the board for an opportunity to leave prison and contribute positively to society, stating she was not the same person she was 32 years ago. However, the boys' father, David Smith, attended the hearing to strongly oppose her release, telling the board that granting parole would represent a severe injustice to his sons, Michael and Alex. Former prosecutor Tommy Pope also testified against her release, arguing that Smith committed the murders out of selfishness to pursue a relationship and continued to seek public attention while incarcerated.

Smith's defense attorney and a psychologist testified that her mental health issues had been stabilized through medication and life changes, arguing she posed a low risk of reoffending. Despite these arguments, the board denied her request. Smith will be eligible to apply for parole again in 2028.

In October 1994, Smith strapped her 3-year-old son Michael and 14-month-old son Alex into her car and allowed the vehicle to roll into John D. Long Lake in Union County, South Carolina. For nine days, she falsely claimed to authorities and national media that an unidentified Black man had carjacked her vehicle with the children inside before eventually confessing to the drownings. A jury convicted her of murder in 1995 and sentenced her to life in prison, which under state law at the time allowed her to become eligible for parole after serving 30 years.
