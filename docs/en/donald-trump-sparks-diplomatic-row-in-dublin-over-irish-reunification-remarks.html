Donald Trump Sparks Diplomatic Row in Dublin Over Irish Reunification Remarks

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

DUBLIN — U.S. President Donald Trump sparked international diplomatic controversy during an official visit to Ireland on Saturday, September 12, 2026, after publicly declaring support for a unified Ireland and hinting at a potential policy shift regarding the Falkland Islands.

Speaking in Dublin following a meeting with Irish Prime Minister Micheál Martin, Trump stated that he would love to see the reunification of the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland, describing the prospect as fantastic and predicting it would happen sooner or later. His comments drew swift pushback from British political figures and Northern Irish unionists. Democratic Unionist Party leader Gavin Robinson stated that the constitutional future of Northern Ireland will be decided by its people rather than by officials in foreign capitals, while the U.K. government reiterated its commitment to the principle of consent under existing peace accords. During his visit, Trump also urged a resumption of oil drilling in the North Sea to offset rising energy costs and indicated that his administration is reviewing Washington's neutral stance on the Falkland Islands sovereignty dispute, according to BBC News. Meanwhile, thousands of demonstrators marched through central Dublin to protest Trump's visit, raising concerns over U.S. foreign policy in the Middle East, while Irish President Catherine Connolly stated she conveyed to Trump the Irish people's firm belief that the normalization of war can never be accepted.

Northern Ireland remained part of the United Kingdom following Irish independence in 1922, leading to decades of political and sectarian conflict known as The Troubles. The landmark 1998 Good Friday Agreement brought an end to the widespread violence by establishing a power-sharing arrangement and stipulating that any future change to Northern Ireland's constitutional status can only occur through a democratic referendum approved by its residents.
