Argentina Assesses Trump Mediation Offer on Falklands as Milei Cancels London Trip

Sunday, September 13, 2026, 00:08 UTC

BUENOS AIRES — Argentine President Javier Milei's administration is evaluating potential U.S. diplomatic involvement in the Falkland Islands dispute following remarks by Donald Trump, while simultaneously canceling a planned presidential trip to London and introducing new legislative sanctions.

The diplomatic developments follow comments made by U.S. President Donald Trump during a visit to Dublin, where he noted that the ongoing dispute between Argentina and the United Kingdom could lead to a potential disaster and expressed willingness to help resolve it if called upon. Argentine officials welcomed the statements as a key signal to keep the sovereignty claim on the international agenda. Amid heightened tensions and firm pushback from British Prime Minister Andy Burnham in Parliament, President Milei decided to suspend a planned October visit to London intended for an investment forum, as reported by Infobae. Furthermore, the Argentine executive branch is preparing to submit the Defense of National Sovereignty Bill to Congress, designed to impose stricter penalties on foreign firms engaging in unauthorized economic activities around the islands.

President Milei is scheduled to address the United Nations General Assembly in New York on September 22, where he plans to reiterate Argentina's claim in accordance with UN resolutions. In parallel with its diplomatic track, Argentina is expanding its military and logistical footprint in Tierra del Fuego through the development of the Integrated Naval Base in Ushuaia and enhanced South Atlantic surveillance systems.
