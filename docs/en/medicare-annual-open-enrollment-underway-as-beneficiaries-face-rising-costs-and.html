Medicare Annual Open Enrollment Underway as Beneficiaries Face Rising Costs and Plan Changes

Sunday, November 22, 2026, 19:45 UTC · 5 sources

Annual open enrollment is currently underway through Dec. 7 for Medicare, the federal program providing health insurance to roughly 70 million beneficiaries across the United States. Enrollees must navigate complex choices between traditional Medicare and private Medicare Advantage plans ahead of coverage taking effect on Jan. 1.

Medicare beneficiaries are split between traditional Medicare, administered by the federal government, and privately offered Medicare Advantage plans. Traditional Medicare covers hospital and doctor visits but lacks an out-of-pocket spending cap and excludes most drug, vision, and dental coverage, leading many to purchase standalone Part D drug plans and Medigap policies. Medicare Advantage offers bundled coverage but typically imposes restricted provider networks and prior authorization rules. According to analysis by health policy research nonprofit KFF, enrollees who remain in their standalone prescription drug plan will experience an average premium increase of 50 percent, rising from $34 to $51 per month. About 10.4 million beneficiaries could see higher premiums, while an estimated 4.3 million people must select new coverage after private insurers discontinued certain Medicare Advantage or prescription drug plans. The changes come as federal caps on out-of-pocket drug costs take effect alongside the end of temporary federal subsidies designed to curb premium increases.

Enrollees whose Medicare Advantage plans are not renewed will be automatically enrolled in traditional Medicare if they take no action. Healthcare experts advise beneficiaries to review annual change notices, verify doctor networks, and seek free, unbiased plan counseling through the State Health Insurance Assistance Program to avoid potential late-enrollment penalties and unexpected out-of-pocket expenses.
