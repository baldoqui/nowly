COP31 Climate Summit Collapses Over Disputes Regarding Fossil Fuel Phase-Out

Sunday, November 22, 2026, 19:45 UTC · 1 source

The United Nations COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Turkey, concluded without a final consensus on Sunday, November 22, after negotiations extended two days past their deadline. The talks collapsed after the European Union and allied nations rejected a draft agreement that omitted commitments to transition away from fossil fuels.

COP31 President Murat Kurum, Turkey's environment minister, confirmed the lack of consensus, stating that negotiators had exhausted all available options. The proposed final text, known as the Antalya Pact and drafted jointly by the Turkish and Australian presidencies, outlined measures to triple climate adaptation funding for vulnerable nations and expand economic electrification. However, it failed to include explicit language committing signatories to reduce fossil fuel dependency, according to reporting by CNN.

The draft deal faced opposition from key negotiators including the European Union, Norway, Panama, and the Marshall Islands. Irish Environment Minister Darragh O’Brien emphasized that the EU could not endorse a final outcome that completely bypassed the issue of fossil fuels. Additionally, climate advocacy groups criticized the draft's emphasis on carbon capture technologies, arguing that relying on carbon capture without active fossil fuel reduction fails to address the root causes of global warming.

Despite the overarching failure to secure a full agreement, negotiation officials noted minor achievements during the summit, including a pledge supported by more than 90 countries to advance global electrification efforts.

The failure in Antalya marks the first major collapse of a UN climate summit since the COP15 conference in Copenhagen in 2009. The breakdown underscores long-standing geopolitical divisions between nations advocating for aggressive climate mitigation policies and major fossil fuel producers resisting mandates that restrict energy production. While the impasse prevents new consensus measures from taking effect, previous commitments established under prior United Nations climate frameworks remain active.
