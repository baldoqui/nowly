Trump Calls Unified Ireland 'Fantastic' During Dublin Visit

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

U.S. President Donald Trump stated during a visit to Dublin on Saturday that a unified Ireland would be 'fantastic,' weighing in on a deeply sensitive political issue during a weekend trip to attend a tournament at his Irish golf resort.

Speaking alongside Irish Prime Minister Micheal Martin, Trump expressed support for the reunification of the Republic of Ireland and British-governed Northern Ireland, suggesting that the merger is inevitable. As reported by BBC Mundo, Trump acknowledged that the United Kingdom would have a say in any future status changes while maintaining that reunification would be a positive outcome. During his two-day diplomatic stop, Trump also met with Irish President Catherine Connolly, who criticized U.S. Middle East policies, while thousands of demonstrators gathered in Dublin to protest his visit before he traveled to his golf course in Doonbeg.

The political division of Ireland dates back to 1921, when six northern counties remained part of the United Kingdom, leading to decades of sectarian violence known as 'The Troubles' that claimed roughly 3,600 lives. Peace was established through the 1998 Good Friday Agreement, which stipulates that a border poll on unification may only be called if a majority in Northern Ireland appears likely to support it. U.S. leaders have historically maintained strict neutrality on Northern Irish sovereignty.
