Argentine Exports Surged Nearly 50% Between 2023 and Mid-2026 Driven by Energy and Mining, PwC Report Shows

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

BUENOS AIRES — Argentine exports grew by 47.8% between 2023 and the first semester of 2026, driven primarily by significant expansions in the energy and mining sectors, according to a study by consulting firm PwC.

The growth was largely fueled by higher export volumes, which rose 48% overall while average export prices declined slightly by 0.1%. According to Noticias Argentinas, fuels and energy led sector growth with a 101.7% increase in export value and a 102.8% jump in volume, while primary products rose 74%. Specific complexes saw dramatic increases, including metalliferous mining and lithium, which rose 227.9% in value, and petroleum and petrochemicals, which climbed 104.5%. Lithium carbonate export value surged by 2,062% due to a 3,198% surge in volume despite lower global prices. The automotive sector was the only major segment to record a decline, falling by 2.1%.

The report notes that Argentina's share of global merchandise exports expanded from 0.29% in 2023 to 0.34% in 2024 and 0.37% in 2025, though it remains below its historic 2011 peak. The sector expansion relies on long-term investments in key assets such as the Vaca Muerta shale field and various mining projects established prior to the analyzed timeframe.
