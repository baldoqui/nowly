President Claudia Sheinbaum Highlights Social Programs and Infrastructure Projects During Yucatán Visit

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

MEXICO CITY — Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo announced during a visit to Mérida, Yucatán, on September 12, 2026, that federal welfare and infrastructure programs have significantly boosted regional development, stating that 90 percent of the population currently feels proud to be Mexican compared to 58 percent in 1990.

Speaking at the Siglo XXI Convention Center, Sheinbaum reported that 781,215 people in Yucatán receive federal welfare benefits representing an investment exceeding 17 billion pesos. According to Quadratín Yucatán, these programs encompass senior pensions, disability allowances, youth employment initiatives, and the Salud Casa por Casa health strategy. In addition to social spending, Sheinbaum detailed major public infrastructure undertakings, including the Maya Train cargo line, federal highway maintenance, two CFE combined-cycle power plants, over 72,000 housing units, and new medical centers such as the Dr. Agustín O'Horán General Hospital. Yucatán Governor Joaquín Díaz Mena commended the federal administration for supporting key state developments including the modernization of Progreso Port and new university campuses.

The presentation in Mérida reflects ongoing federal development priorities across southeastern Mexico, focusing on expanded healthcare access, transport infrastructure, and higher education institutions such as the Rosario Castellanos University campus in Kanasín.
