U.S. Health Officials Declare End to Historic Cyclospora Outbreak

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

U.S. health officials have officially declared the end of the largest Cyclospora outbreak in national history, which sickened nearly 13,000 people and caused two deaths across 21 states after being linked to contaminated iceberg lettuce from Mexico.

According to the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, the outbreak resulted in 12,883 confirmed cases and 1,043 hospitalizations out of more than 19,000 total cyclosporiasis infections reported nationwide since May 1. The health emergency led to a July 17 recall of iceberg lettuce grown in central Mexico and processed by Taylor Farms, which was distributed to major restaurant chains including Taco Bell, KFC, and Pizza Hut, as well as regional supermarkets. Michigan was the hardest-hit state, recording the highest number of reported cases and both fatalities, which occurred in individuals with severe underlying health conditions. Although federal officials confirm that the recalled lettuce is no longer on the market and on-site inspections in Mexico have concluded, the exact mechanism of fecal contamination remains undetermined.

Cyclosporiasis is an intestinal infection caused by the microscopic parasite Cyclospora cayetanensis, which spreads through food or water contaminated with human feces and causes severe watery diarrhea, cramps, and nausea. Outbreaks typically peak in late spring and summer, but the scale of infections this year far exceeded the previous record set in 2019, when approximately 4,700 cases were reported nationwide.
