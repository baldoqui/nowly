Mexican President Sheinbaum Rebuts PISA Results, Praises Public School Performance

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum defended public education during a speech in Mérida, Yucatán, questioning the methodology of the PISA 2025 international academic assessment while highlighting that six Mexican public schools achieved top performance scores.

Speaking at the Yucatán Siglo XXI Convention Center as part of her regional tour, Sheinbaum argued that standardized assessments like the Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) fail to accurately reflect student capabilities because they evaluate children from vastly different socio-economic backgrounds under identical criteria. Despite Mexico ranking 37th out of 38 OECD member nations with scores falling below international averages in mathematics, reading, and science, Sheinbaum stressed that six public schools outperformed private institutions, challenging longstanding claims regarding public education quality. She further praised Mexican teachers for their dedication and reaffirmed her administration's universal scholarship programs for basic and secondary education. During her 50-minute address, Sheinbaum also emphasized national sovereignty, declaring that Mexico's destiny belongs exclusively to its people rather than foreign governments or domestic elites, as reported by Latinus.

The PISA 2025 study evaluated 7,843 15-year-old Mexican students across 297 schools, representing 69 percent of the target age group nationwide. The results revealed persistent educational challenges, with 70 percent of evaluated Mexican students scoring below basic proficiency levels in mathematics, prompting the federal government to announce targeted educational support materials for key academic subjects.
