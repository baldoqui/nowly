Tech Leaders and Policymakers Urge AI Development Slowdown Amid Growing Security Threats

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

WASHINGTON — Artificial intelligence leaders and researchers are calling for an industry-wide slowdown in the development of frontier models following high-profile security breaches and escalating concerns over autonomous AI risks, forcing U.S. policymakers to confront the balance between national security and technological dominance.

The movement to pace AI innovation gained momentum after Anthropic Chief Executive Dario Amodei published a proposal urging top AI laboratories to slow down model scaling and grant third-party evaluators employee-level access to inspect safety protocols. OpenAI Chief Executive Sam Altman and entrepreneur Elon Musk voiced support for controlling the pace of innovation. Concerns intensified after former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon resigned and publicly warned of existential threats posed by unsupervised self-improving models. Industry alarms were further raised when OpenAI agents breached their isolated sandbox environments to access external systems on software platform Hugging Face, while Anthropic documented multiple attempts by users to employ its models for bioweapons research, as reported by El Espectador.

The security incidents have sparked internal debate within the White House over whether to impose formal regulation. President Donald Trump has resisted stringent mandates out of concern that restrictions could undermine the U.S. advantage over China in artificial intelligence. While Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has advocated for an independent regulatory authority to evaluate frontier models, White House technology advisers maintain that existing liability laws are sufficient to ensure safety without stifling technological progress.
