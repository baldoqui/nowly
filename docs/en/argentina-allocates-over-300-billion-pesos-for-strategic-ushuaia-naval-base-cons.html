Argentina Allocates Over 300 Billion Pesos for Strategic Ushuaia Naval Base Construction

September 13, 2026 · Spanish sources

The Argentine government announced it will allocate more than 300 billion pesos in the 2027 national budget to build and operationalize the Integrated Naval Base in Ushuaia, Tierra del Fuego, aiming to complete the self-funded state project by 2029.

Defense Minister Carlos Presti and Foreign Minister Pablo Quirno confirmed during a site visit to Ushuaia that the deep-south naval facility will be funded entirely through national state resources without reliance on external loans or multilateral organizations. According to LA NACION, official estimates place the total project cost between 400 million and 500 million dollars, with initial works scheduled to begin in early 2027. Construction will be executed in phased public tenders over 30 to 36 months, featuring a 585-meter pier capable of accommodating Antarctic logistics vessels, maritime service buildings, and site urbanization. During their visit to the Fueguino capital, the cabinet members evaluated terrain logistics alongside Armed Forces technical personnel and held a meeting with Tierra del Fuego Governor Gustavo Melella, who expressed readiness to collaborate with the federal project.

Conceived decades ago as a gateway to Antarctica and a bolster for South Atlantic sovereignty, the project saw a ceremonial foundation stone laid in 2022 that remained undeveloped. The revitalized initiative also includes infrastructure upgrades to the Petrel Base runway in Antarctica, intended to strengthen Argentina's logistical projection and sovereign positioning in the far south.
