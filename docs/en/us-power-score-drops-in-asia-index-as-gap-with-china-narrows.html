US Power Score Drops in Asia Index as Gap with China Narrows

Sunday, November 22, 2026, 19:45 UTC · 5 sources

The foreign policy sway of the United States in Asia has posted its sharpest decline in the nine-year history of the Lowy Institute's Asia Power Index, narrowing the power gap between Washington and Beijing, according to the annual report released on Monday.

While the United States continues to lead China in six of the survey's eight criteria, it now trails Beijing in diplomatic influence and economic relationships, according to Australian Associated Press. China is also closing the gap in military capability. Lowy Institute researcher Jack Sato stated that the shift is primarily driven by American declines rather than Chinese expansion, noting that US focus was diverted to the conflict in Iran. So-called middle powers such as Australia, Japan, and South Korea also underperformed expectations to assume broader regional security roles, remaining focused on domestic priorities. Sato highlighted growing bilateral defense coordination between nations like Australia, Japan, and the Philippines, but warned that deepening alignment among China, Russia, and North Korea remains a primary security risk.

The report follows Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese's recent diplomatic engagements across Asia, including signing joint military exercise agreements with Quad partners and securing bilateral energy deals to offset global economic disruptions caused by foreign conflicts.
