California Business Owner Pursues Rare Fraud Lawsuit Against Merchant Cash Advance Industry

Sunday, November 22, 2026, 19:00 UTC · 5 sources

GLENDALE, Calif. -- Vahe Shahinian, owner of Los Angeles-based event ticketing platform It's My Seat, is taking alternative lender EIN Cap to trial in U.S. District Court in Los Angeles over allegations of fraud following a 2019 financing transaction that depleted his business and forced the foreclosure of his home. According to court filings and statements from Shahinian, a broker deceptively pitched a low-interest $750,000 line of credit that turned out to be a predatory merchant cash advance requiring daily bank withdrawals of $3,600.

Merchant cash advances, known as MCAs, are fast-growing financing products that purchase a business's future revenue in exchange for upfront cash. Because they are legally structured as sales rather than loans, MCA providers operate largely without federal or state lending regulations, assessing annual fee rates between 25% and 200%. Shahinian sought capital in 2018 to fund an international concert tour after American Express discontinued a short-term merchant loan program he relied upon. Desperate for quick liquidity, he accepted an offer from broker middlemen associated with Wall Street-based EIN Cap, believing daily withdrawals were a temporary demonstration of creditworthiness required to unlock a long-term low-interest credit line. The credit line never arrived, draining Shahinian's reserves and forcing him into a cascade of secondary high-cost advances, over $1 million in personal debt to family and friends, and eventual foreclosure on his Glendale property. In legal proceedings, EIN Cap denied Shahinian's claims, arguing in court documents that it delivered only the merchant cash advance specified in its contract and attributing any misleading statements to independent brokers. Investigations by Shahinian and his attorney revealed that the broker operating under the alias Bryan Stein was previously convicted of grand larceny under the name Boris Shteyngart. Further legal records show EIN Cap has faced litigation in at least nine states over debt collection actions and insolvent borrower claims, including an Illinois federal court ruling requiring the firm to disgorge profits derived from a Ponzi-like scheme.

Small business reliance on high-cost alternative funding has expanded significantly across the United States as traditional lenders and Small Business Administration programs enforce stricter credit requirements. In response to mounting bankruptcies and industry abuses, California and several other states have enacted legislation requiring clearer fee disclosures, with new California licensing and inspection laws for MCA brokers scheduled to take effect in 2028. Shahinian continues to pursue his lawsuit to seek more than $5 million in accrued debt and damages while establishing accountability for high-cost business financing practices.
